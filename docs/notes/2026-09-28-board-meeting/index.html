SASD Board Meeting Notes — September 28, 2026

Executive summary

The Board completed a short regular meeting focused on annual behavior/PBIS reporting, facilities progress, preliminary 2026–27 financial actions, and routine consent items. The Board adopted the agenda, approved the consent agenda, approved the preliminary budget for presentation at the October 20 Budget Hearing, approved a preliminary $15.56 million levy for the same hearing, approved an up-to-$2.5 million cash-flow borrowing resolution, and adjourned.

The most substantive discussion concerned student-behavior response practices and facility progress. Administration reported 21 seclusion/restraint incidents in 2025–26 involving 8 students: 12 seclusions and 9 restraints. Board questions focused on when an entire class is moved during a student incident, the effect on instructional time, response staffing, and whether data on such classroom removals can be tracked or compared. Administration said moving a regulated class can be safer and legally preferable to physically moving a dysregulated student unless statutory emergency thresholds are met; the district does not currently have a single exact count for those classroom removals.

Facilities were described as generally on track. New security systems are operating; Tech Ed/ARC spaces are expected to begin receiving students toward the end of October, although furniture and some heavy-duty tables remain delayed. Weather delayed northwest-parking-lot utility work, but the projected parking-lot timing remains around Thanksgiving. Administration discussed possible community access/open-house opportunities for new SHS spaces in November and a Herman Elementary public open house on October 30.

Financial actions were explicitly preliminary. Staff said budget and levy totals will be updated as pupil-count, open-enrollment, equalization-aid, and Revenue Limit data become final, with the key state information due October 15. The cash-flow note is contingency authority, not an expected borrowing event; staff said the district has not needed cash-flow borrowing in many years.

  • 2025–26 data: 21 total incidents involving 8 students.
    • 12 seclusions.
    • 9 restraints.
    • Southside ELC: 7 seclusions / 3 restraints, involving 4 students.

  • Rationale: physically moving the regulated class can be safer and less escalatory than physically moving a dysregulated student; statutory thresholds limit when staff may use hands-on intervention. [00:09:59–00:10:50]

  • The Board discussed whether building-level tracking could document how often class removals occur and the instructional impact. Administration said principals/teachers could track the data; no districtwide exact count was presented. [00:10:52–00:11:17; 00:14:21–00:14:54]
