## Reports and discussion

### 2026 Summer School Report

**Approx. 00:01:00–00:10:20**

Dr. Wendy Burnett reported on the District’s summer programming:

- **918 students** enrolled in summer classes, plus **496 swim-lesson registrations**. Some students may be counted in both groups.
- Staffing included **86 certified staff** and **51 classified staff**.
- Camp Sparta offered **35 classes in 83 sections**.
- Programming included Camp Sparta; all-day and after-school WIN/intervention; middle- and high-school credit recovery; credit-bearing courses; SVLA courses; Spartan Fit/Speed and Strength; STEMKAMP; swim lessons; and Extended School Year (ESY) services.
- ESY operated in Southside, Herrman, and Sparta High School. Summer programming was available to district-resident students, including those attending private, virtual, homeschool, or open-enrollment programs.
- Reading intervention data:

**86 students** signed up.
Data were available for **57 students** because some did not attend regularly.
**14 students** who began below the 25th percentile reached that benchmark during the summer.
More than half of students with data improved reading scores.
- Facilities were used across Southside, Herrman, Sparta High School, and Meadowview; Meadowview accommodated overflow due to the unavailability of the LF gym.
- Staff plan to update summer-course outcome summaries and standards alignment beginning in winter.

**Board discussion**

- A Board member asked how ESY eligibility is determined and whether participation is required.
- Administration stated that ESY is an **IEP-team decision** for students at risk of significant regression and significant difficulty regaining skills after breaks.
- Parents may decline ESY even when a student is found eligible; the decision must be documented.

### Sparta High School Fall Co-Curricular Report

**Approx. 00:20:00–00:45:10**

Mr. Dahl presented fall athletics and co-curricular highlights.

**Athletics and activities**

- **Cross country:** 18 participants; girls finished seventh and boys fifth in conference. Scholar-athlete percentages were deferred until first-quarter grades are finalized.
- **Football:** 64 athletes; **3–6**; seventh in the MVC. Administration described the team as talented but young.
- **Girls golf:** 11 athletes; fourth in the MVC. The report described a fourth-place conference-meet finish and a sectional qualifier.
- **Boys soccer:** 44 athletes; ninth in the MVC.
- **Girls tennis:** sixth in the conference.
- **Volleyball:** 45 athletes; **17–14**; fifth in the MVC. The team defeated Central in the first playoff round after being swept by Central in regular-season play, then lost to the number-two seed at Chippewa Falls.
- **Adaptive Sports League:** The soccer season included three events as the program develops capacity. The group raised **52 pounds of food** for the Hunger Task Force and had **10 volunteers** at a home event.
- **Cheer:** Participation has grown by roughly 20 participants in each of the last two years, according to the report. The program plans to try competitive events for the first time.
- **Marching band:** **70 members**; fourth at Waukesha West, first at Greendale with Best Color Guard and Best Percussion, and fifth in Class AA at state. The program had **27 parent volunteers**.

**Community and youth involvement**
