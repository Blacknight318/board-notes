## Budget hearing and public discussion

**Audio:** approximately 01:10–02:13

### Fund balance, special education, and purchased services

- Electors asked about reserve/fund-balance use, budget shortfalls, purchased services, open enrollment, and special-education reimbursement.
- Administration explained that Wisconsin’s special-education reimbursement is not full reimbursement. The meeting book states that the statutory reimbursement level was raised to 40%, while practical reimbursement can be lower because the appropriation is fixed across districts.
- A participant requested comparative data on special-education costs/services and other districts; administration indicated that additional information could be provided.

### Community Service Fund and programming

- Discussion addressed the Community Service Levy and programs such as open gym and school-resource-officer services.
- Administration explained that reducing a Community Service Fund program would reduce the community-service levy and therefore affect local property taxes, but would not directly increase operating-budget capacity.
- Participants debated whether certain community programs should continue and whether individual programs could be removed through an appropriate budget motion.

### Facilities, enrollment, and district improvement

- An elector questioned the relationship between declining enrollment, the expanded/high-school facility footprint, ongoing maintenance costs, and strategies to improve the district and attract/retain families.
- Administration and speakers discussed the difference between capital-project funding and operating funds, as well as the financial constraints created by the state revenue-limit structure.

### Student experience, behavior, and staffing

- Public comments included concerns about student behavior, bullying, classroom disruption, student safety, administrative response, and the need to support teachers and educational assistants.
- Speakers emphasized the value of classroom staff and expressed concern that financial discussions should not overlook the impact of staffing and direct student support.
